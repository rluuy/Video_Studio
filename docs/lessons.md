# Lessons

One line each, newest last in its group. Added to after every video. A lesson that a program can check becomes a check.

## Order of work

- Voice before animation. Clip P was animated to planned seconds, and 16 line times were then moved by hand.
- Draft renders in 1080p, one 4K render at the end. Clip P was rendered in full five times and stopped twice.
- Freeze the words before the pictures. Every change of wording after the voice costs credits and timing.
- Decide what is real data first. It is the decision that is expensive to change.
- Write down a decision with its default. "Defaults for everything" was Ryan's whole answer once.

## Pictures

- The depth removed limits the exaggeration. At x 20,000 clip A's slab would have been twice as tall as wide, so it is x 10,000.
- Surfaces are shaded over several pixels when a fine ripple sparkles, with heights and colors exact.
- A surface and its rim need values beyond the aperture. Continue the map outward before drawing.
- Footprints drawn upward need a note, or a dent reads as a bump.
- A focus that should become a point does not, when the tool is wider than the error. Draw what the computation gives.
- A veil made before the overlays does not cover them. Make it last (`film.js` gives it the top layer).
- A cut-out footprint on a coarse grid has a saw-toothed edge. Compute it on a finer grid and spread the edge.
- Labels of two angles collide while the angles are still small. Let them in late.

## Data

- Row 0 of a height map is the largest y. Kernels are not always square. MATLAB arrays are column by column, so a vector
  of dwell times needs order F or its rows scramble.
- Stamping a footprint is the planner's own sum. Check it against the recorded result before drawing it.
- A plan's removal carries a tilt that the grader removes. During a carve, take each pixel's share off in proportion, or a
  false tilt appears.
- Say what a color is relative to. "Still to remove" against the finished surface reads better than against a moving mean.

## Voice

- George speaks at about 2.5 words per second, slower on short lines with commas, 1.9 to 3.1 in extremes.
- Credits are about 0.4 per character with this model. The account page lags some minutes behind.
- Every line is a file named by its text. Never delete `voice/`.
- Claude cannot hear. Nobody had listened to clip P for a while after it was done, so ask Ryan to.
- A changed respelling makes a new take of every line with that name.

## Programs

- Chrome now and then antialiases a letter or an edge differently from run to run. Compare with a retry.
- The screenshot, not the drawing, sets the render time at 4K. JPEG at quality 100 halves it.
- Cutting a copied stream by time overshoots by a few frames. Count frames from keyframe to keyframe.
- Encoder keyframes nudge a resting picture. A scan for rests has to forgive one frame.
- Long Bash commands with many here-documents fail on this laptop, and backslashes in them are halved. Write files with
  the Write tool.
- PowerPoint: `PlayOnEntry` alone waits for a click, so the play effect's trigger is set too. `Quit` closes Ryan's own
  PowerPoint if it is open. `swap_deck_media.ps1` handles both.

## People

- Review agents are confident when wrong. "The arrowheads are rotated" was an equilateral triangle that shows no direction.
- A reviewer who knows the earlier findings looks for those. Start fresh each time.
- Fixes made after a review cause new faults. In the first test of the skills, an arrow added in round 1 was the must-fix of
  round 2. The last change always needs a fresh look.
- Reviewers of the pictures find fault with words that are already approved. Ask for them at the script.
- Ryan edits the deck. Never rebuild it.
